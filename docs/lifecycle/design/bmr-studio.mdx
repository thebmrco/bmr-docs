# <Icon name="palette" size="1em" /> BMR Studio

<PageIntro illustration="design-studio" meta={['Web platform, on a computer']}>

Design the room in 3D. **Pick a mode, and the Studio gives you the tools for that job.**

</PageIntro>

<Notice kind="design" />

## <Icon name="add-new" size="1em" /> Starting a design {#starting-a-design}

In a room's **Designs** tab, press **New Design**. On a scan, **Turn into Design** opens the same dialog with that scan chosen. Name the design and choose what it is **based on**:

| Based on | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Scan** | The room as scanned (walls, floor, doors, windows and openings), furnished with a model for each object found |
| **Existing Design** | A copy of another design in the room |
| **Custom Room** | A rectangular room from its length, width and height, to shape in the Studio |
| **Cisco file** | A room from Cisco Workspace Designer or the Video Room Calculator. It appears once an Admin has turned on **Cisco room import**. See [Import a Cisco room](../../integrations/cisco-vrc#import-a-cisco-room) |

**Furnished** means each object the scan found becomes one model of its kind: your organisation's own, set under [Scan models](../../structure/organisation#scan-models), or else a generic one. It is coloured like the real object where a marker photo or the scan's mesh shows its colour. What doesn't belong in a meeting room, such as a bed or a kitchen appliance, is left out.

A scanned **display** with no model of yours is a screen scaled to the size the scan found, with a cart under it if it stands on the floor. The [BOM](#bom) counts the screen as a generic display with its size, for example **Generic display 65"**.

### The welcome {#the-welcome}

The first time a design from a scan, or a Custom Room, opens in the Studio, **Welcome to BMR Studio** asks how to start. Pick one and press **Start**.

| Start | What the design holds |
|---|---|
| **Placeholder blocks** | The scan's objects as simple blocks, coloured by type |
| **Generic equipment** | The scan's objects as models, as described above |
| **Remove everything** | Only the room: walls, floor, doors, windows and openings |
| **Start from a default room configuration** | The empty room with the setup you pick placed in it. **Show** opens the list. Those sized for the room's floor area come first, marked **Recommended**. Each says whose it is: **Your organisation**, **Shared with you** or **BMR default**. See [Default Room Configuration](default-room-configuration) |

A **Custom Room** has no scanned objects, so it offers **Empty room**, with the configurations already open.

You can't skip the question. **Exit**, or **Esc**, asks **Exit BMR Studio?** and leaves the Studio, and the welcome is there again the next time you open the design. Copies of a design, imported Cisco files and older designs don't ask.

:::info Automatic design from scans
An Admin can have every new room scan make a design by itself: **Preferences › General › Automatic design from scans**. It is off by default. The design is named **From scan** and the date, is furnished as above, and becomes the room's **Active** design, in place of the current one. Until it is there, a few minutes after the scan arrives, the room says **New scan added, design being generated**. Its first open in the Studio shows the welcome. A scan that arrives while the setting is off never gets one, even if you turn it on later. See [Organisation](../../structure/organisation#general).
:::

## <Icon name="scan" size="1em" /> Modes: how the Studio works {#-modes}

Every job has its own **mode**. Switch at the bottom of the screen. The mode decides:

- **what you can select and change.** Everything else stays visible, but locked.
- **which tabs the sidebar shows**, and which tools you get.

The modes read left to right, as the steps a room is designed in. A green arrow marks the current one and slides along when the mode changes, whichever way it changed. Where there is little room, the strip shows icons only.

## <Icon name="eye" size="1em" /> View {#view}

Look around and present. Items can't be dragged here, so it's safe for reviews. Select anything to read its [properties](#properties). If you can edit the design, **Edit** and **Delete** appear above the item: see [Editing](#editing).

The controls for looking sit together in the middle of the toolbar, in every mode:

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **2D · Split · 3D** | The floor plan, the 3D room, or both side by side. **Split** needs a wide screen and is in Cables mode for now. |
| <Icon name="sliders-horizontal" size="1em" /> **View settings** | What the room shows, and how it is lit. See [View settings](#view-settings). |
| <Icon name="radar" size="1em" /> **Coverage** | What the cameras, microphones, displays and loudspeakers reach. See [Coverage](#coverage). |
| <Icon name="video" size="1em" /> **Camera** | Preset views (**Overview**, **Top**, four **Sides**) and your saved ones. **In room** (View mode) walks the room at eye height, **Camera view** sees what a device camera sees, **Center camera on object** frames your selection. See [Moving the camera](#camera). |

Next to them: **Render mode** (View mode only, see [Render modes](#render-modes)), **Measure**, and **Markers** from the scan once the room has some. The **Presentation tab** saves views to [present](#presenting-a-design).

Above the modes, in 3D and in 2D:

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Preset** | The arrows step through the preset views: **Overview**, **Top** and four **Sides**. A design opens in a view of its own, turned to the walls, so no preset is named until you pick one. Not in 2D. |
| **Focus** | Centres on what is selected. With nothing selected it goes to **Overview** (in 2D, to the whole plan). |
| **Zoom** | **+** and **−**, with the level between them. **100%** is the whole design in view. |

- **Center camera on object** (in the **Camera** menu), or the **F** key, shows the selected object whole, with space around it, in the part of the screen no panel covers. **F** with nothing selected fits the whole design.
- The camera looks at the object head-on, from the side it faces: a wall from inside the room, equipment from the front, a ceiling item from below, a table from the side. It stays inside the room, so a long wall in a narrow room fills the screen edge to edge. Small items come with about 1.5 m of their surroundings.
- From the 3D view, picking a device in **Camera view** flies the view into it, and out again when you leave. **Back to 3D** (or **Esc**) returns.
- **Camera view** lists one entry per camera device and looks through its widest lens. Zoom in up to the lens's optical × digital zoom. The view pans and tilts within the lens's range.

**Compass.** At the top right of the 3D view. Its needle turns with the view, so the red half always points north. Click **N**, **E**, **S** or **W** to look that way, also in a shared view. It is greyed out when the room was scanned without a compass bearing. The scan's 3D viewer shows the same compass, but doesn't turn the view.

### Coverage {#coverage}

<Icon name="radar" size="1em" /> shows what the devices in the design reach, one type at a time: **None** (the default), **Display**, **Microphone**, **Speaker** <FeatureToggleTag /> or **Camera**.

| Pick | What it draws |
|---|---|
| **Display** | Where the screen stays legible for detailed content, from the AVIXA DISCAS viewing-distance rule |
| **Microphone** | How far each microphone picks up speech at full level, and where it starts to fall off |
| **Speaker** <FeatureToggleTag /> | Which chairs the loudspeakers reach evenly. See [Loudspeaker coverage](#speaker-coverage) |
| **Camera** | Each camera device's field of view, with all its lenses together, as far as its farthest lens. What is inside it is framed at the far end |

#### Loudspeaker coverage <FeatureToggleTag /> {#speaker-coverage}

<FeatureToggle />

Do the chairs hear the loudspeakers evenly? Each chair is rated against the best-served chair, on direct sound only (no reflections), at 4 kHz.

- **Green**: within 6 dB of the best chair. Speech is clear there.
- **Red**: more than 6 dB below it. Speech is harder to understand and listening takes more effort.

The bar under the title says **Recommended coverage** and counts the chairs: *n covered*, *n not covered*. The floor shows where the sound is within 6 dB of the best chair. Without chairs, the area is drawn from the loudest place in the room.

<Figure
  src="/img/design/studio-speaker-coverage.jpg"
  alt="A meeting room with chairs and loudspeakers in BMR Studio. A bar over the room reads Loudspeaker coverage, then Recommended coverage, 13 covered, 4 not covered and Details. The floor is orange, and green rings mark the covered chairs and red rings the others"
  variant="inset"
  maxWidth={500}
  caption="Loudspeaker coverage: 13 chairs covered, 4 not."
/>

**Details** opens a second bar and draws **rings** on the floor: thin lines 3, 6, 9 and 12 dB below the best chair.

<Figure
  src="/img/design/studio-speaker-coverage-details.jpg"
  alt="The same room with Details open. A second bar shows 1 kHz and 4 kHz, with 4 kHz picked, Evenness uneven and Data estimated, and thin orange rings are drawn on the floor"
  variant="inset"
  maxWidth={500}
  caption="The same room with Details open."
/>

In the second bar:

| | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Frequency** | **1 kHz** or **4 kHz**: what the picture is drawn for. **4 kHz** is the default. Most loudspeakers get narrower as the frequency rises. |
| **Evenness** | With chairs: loudest to quietest chair, as **very even** (up to 3 dB), **even** (up to 6), **noticeably uneven** (up to 8), **uneven** (up to 12) or **very uneven** (more). The classes are the coverage envelope of AVIXA A102.01. |
| **Data** | Where the loudspeakers' patterns come from: **manufacturer**, **estimated** or **none, 90° assumed**. It shows only when a loudspeaker has no manufacturer data, and counts each kind when they mix. |

**manufacturer** is the directivity from the datasheet, which BMR adds for some loudspeakers. **estimated** comes from the coverage angle on the catalog item. With **none**, a 90° angle is assumed. A loudspeaker missing its coverage angle? Tell BMR with [Send Feedback](../../getting-started/send-feedback). How BMR fills it: [Placed devices](../../catalog/ports-and-devices#placed-devices).

In **View** and **Equipment** the **BOM** opens in a panel on the left. It lists **every item in the room**, from a catalog or not. The sections are the [four categories](../discovery/inventory#categories) of the Inventory: **Equipment**, **Infrastructure**, **Accessories** and **Cables**. Each line has the picture, name, category and maker, SKU and quantity. Cables are counted by type, with their lengths, and the ceiling's materials come last.

## <Icon name="photo" size="1em" /> Photoreal rendering <FeatureToggleTag /> {#photoreal}

<FeatureToggle />

A third render mode: a **path-traced**, photo-like picture of the design, with real light, shadows and reflections. Use it to show a customer the finished room, or for a still image. For everyday editing, Standard and High quality are quicker.

| You need | |
|---|---|
| **Photoreal on** for your organisation | Ask your BMR contact |
| **A browser with WebGPU** | Recent Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox. See [WebGPU support](../../getting-started/webgpu#browsers) |
| **The 3D view** | Not the floor plan |

1. In **View** mode, pick **Photoreal** in the toolbar, or in the bar while [presenting](#presenting-a-design).
2. Keep the camera still. The picture refines (*Rendering 12 / 64*), then denoises.
3. Passes and denoising: **View settings** › **Lighting & environment**.

Photoreal missing, won't start or looks grainy? See [WebGPU support › Fix common problems](../../getting-started/webgpu#fix-common-problems).

## <Icon name="equipment" size="1em" /> Equipment {#equipment}

Place and arrange equipment, furniture, outlets, logos and murals. The **Room Configuration** tab offers ready-made setups for the room's size: yours, ones shared with you and BMR's. See [Default Room Configuration](default-room-configuration).

### Equipment tabs {#equipment-tabs}

| Tab | Holds |
|---|---|
| **Generic** | Placeholder items — a display, a chair — when the model doesn't matter yet. |
| **Catalog** | Your organisation's [Equipment Catalog](../../catalog/). |
| **Bundles** <FeatureToggleTag /> | Saved sets of equipment, placed in one go. |
| **Branding** | Your logos and [panel murals](#panel-murals). |

Equipment **snaps onto mounts** (VESA, rack, camera screw) and moves with them. Drag it off to detach.

### Bundles <FeatureToggleTag /> {#bundles}

<FeatureToggle />

A **bundle** is a reusable kit — for example a video bar, display, mount and table microphone.

1. Select **at least two** items in the room.
2. Choose **Create bundle**, name it, tick the **catalogs** and what it **Mounts to**.
3. **Save bundle**. Place it from the **Bundles** tab.

The placed copy is independent: editing the bundle later doesn't change rooms that already use it.

## <Icon name="edit" size="1em" /> Editing {#editing}

Pick an item and its tools stand right above it. They step aside while you drag, resize or rotate it, and come back when you let go. The selection stays when you change mode.

In **View**, if you can edit the design, an item you pick gets two buttons: **Edit**, which opens the mode that edits it, with the item still selected and the camera where it was, and **Delete**, which removes it at once and offers **Undo**. **Del** and undo work there too.

The editing modes give more. Which buttons show depends on the item and the mode. [Cable mode](#cable-mode) has its own tools.

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Center camera on Object** | Frames the item. Also **F**. See [Moving the camera](#camera) |
| **Rotate**, **Resize**, **Reset** | Turn it, change its size, put its rotation back to zero. A wall item goes back square to its wall |
| **Duplicate**, **Repeat** | A copy. A row or a grid of copies: [see below](#arrange) |
| **Select all of type** | Every item like this one: [see below](#select-all-of-type) |
| **Distribute**, **Align in a row**, **Align in a column** | For two or more free-standing items: [see below](#arrange) |
| **Move to mount** | For equipment on a mount: choose another mount it fits |
| **Replace** | Swap it for another item: [see below](#replace) |
| **Split**, **Close gap** | For walls. **Split** cuts a wall in two where you click, **Esc** cancels. **Close gap** draws the wall between two selected walls that don't meet |
| **Group**, **Ungroup** | Several items that select and move as one. **Detach from group** takes a single item out |
| **Create bundle** <FeatureToggleTag /> | Saves the selection as a [bundle](#bundles) |
| **Delete** | Removes the item. **Del** does the same |

### Select all of type {#select-all-of-type}

Pick one item and press **Select all of type**. Every item like it is selected: the same catalog item, the same shape, or the same kind of thing, such as all the walls or all the doors. Only what the current mode lets you pick. Then change, move or delete them together.

A wall that the camera cuts away so you can look into the room is included, and shows see-through while it is selected. Items you hid yourself are not.

### Replace {#replace}

Pick one or more items and press **Replace**. It works on equipment and on scanned objects: chairs, tables, sofas, displays and the rest.

1. The **Equipment** sidebar opens in replace mode, with a banner: **Replacing Lounge chair**, or **Replacing 15 items**, and **Cancel**.
2. Items of the same kind come first, under **Same type: Chairs**, then **Other items**. Items that can't be mounted the way the original is are left out. A mixed selection has no same-type group.
3. **Hover** a card for a moment: the item appears in place of every selected item, so you see it in the room first. Move away and the originals come back.
4. **Click** the card, or its **Replace** icon, to swap.

The new item takes the place, the turn and the mounting of the old one. **Cancel** leaves things as they were.

### Repeat, Distribute and Align {#arrange}

For columns, beams, chairs and anything else that comes in rows.

**Repeat** copies one item into a row. A panel opens on the canvas:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Count & spacing** | **Number in a row**, the original included, and **Spacing** from centre to centre. Direction buttons set which way the row runs. **Bend** curves the row along an arc and turns each item with it |
| **Between walls** | Click the two walls to fill between. The row spreads between them, with the **Gap at the walls** set to **Equal**, **Half** or **None**. If it doesn't fit, the panel says so |
| **Rows**, **Row spacing** | As many rows again, for a grid |

The copies show as grey ghosts until you press **Apply**, which adds them as one undo step. A wall item's row runs along the wall or up it, and an item on a table keeps its height.

**Distribute** spreads two or more free-standing items between two walls you click, with equal gaps and the same **Gap at the walls** choice. **Align in a row** and **Align in a column** line them up along the room's own axes. Each is one undo step.

While you move an item in a row, a gap that is nearly the same as its neighbour's snaps to match it, and equal gaps are drawn as dimension lines with their length, such as **= 2.40 m**. A pillar or beam also shows its gaps to its neighbours and the walls.

### Placing {#placing}

- A floor item snaps **flush against a wall**. With the grid on, it snaps by its **sides**.
- A new wall item lands on a **free part of the wall**, or in front of what covers it.
- Whatever stands between the camera and the selected item turns **see-through**, so you can see where a new item went.
- A warning marks two items that really clash. They must share height as well as floor area, so a laptop on a table or a panel on the ceiling is left out. **View settings › Dimensions & guides › Placement warnings** hides the labels.

### Properties {#properties}

The **Properties** tab is in every mode. It reads name on the left, value on the right: read-only values in grey boxes, editable ones outlined. Sections fold, and the panel remembers which.

**Nothing selected, in View:**

| Section | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Room details** | Room size as length, width and height, floor area, seats, the number of equipment items and the cables |
| **Project details** | Organisation, location, room, the **Design name**, who created it, when it was created and last updated, and what it is based on |

The **Design name** is editable here. It renames the design, as in the toolbar.

**An item selected.** The head shows its picture, name and category. Below: **Name**, **Label**, **Category**, **Brand**, **Colour** and **Mounting**. Only **Label** is yours to edit: it is numbered when the item is placed, and once you edit it, it stays as you wrote it.

**Layout**, open by default, holds the numbers:

- **Dimensions** as L, W and H (T for a wall's thickness, Ø for a round top), **Position** as X, Y and Z (Y only in 3D) and **Rotation**, in metres and degrees. Where they apply: distance from the ceiling, height on the wall and screen content.
- Each number takes a typed value (a comma works as the decimal mark), **↑** and **↓** for 0.01 (**Shift** for 0.1), or a drag on its **letter**, left and right. Dragging a position moves the item as you go and counts as one undo step. A dimension applies when you let go.

Further down, where they apply: the absorber options, a video device's settings, an LED wall, materials and colours, table and shape settings, and outlets.

A generic display has a **Size** from standard diagonals and a **Mounting**: **On the wall** or **On a stand**. When a scan placed it, the tab also shows what the scan measured, and **Check the size** if it found the display wider than usual. Press **Checked** once you have.

## <Icon name="cable" size="1em" /> Cables {#cable-mode}

Cabling starts from **what needs to be connected**, not from drawing cables.

Here you change cables, outlets and boxes. Other equipment stays put: click a device to see what's plugged in.

- **Cables save as you go.**

## <Icon name="copy" size="1em" /> Design versions

A room can have several **designs**, and each design keeps its own **versions**.

### Save and Save as New Design {#automatic-versioning}

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Save** | Saves your changes as a **new version** (*Version 2*, *Version 3* …). Earlier versions are kept. Greyed out while there is nothing to save. |
| **Save as New Design** (in the menu beside **Save**) | Makes a **separate design**, not linked to this one. |

- The **Versions** tab lists every version: **Activate** one to make it current, **Rename** or **Delete** the others.

## <Icon name="monitor" size="1em" /> Presenting a design {#presenting-a-design}

**Present** (Shift + F), next to the modes, takes the design full screen and steps through it one view at a time.

- **Standard views** first (Overview, Floor plan, …), then **your saved views**.
- **← / →** to step, jump to any view from the bar, switch to **High quality** or **[Photoreal](#photoreal)**, **take a screenshot**, **Esc** to stop.
- People you share a design with read-only can present it too.

### Saving your own views

In **View** mode, open the **Presentation** tab, set up the camera and click **Save current view**. Views can be renamed, reordered, replaced or removed.

## <Icon name="circle-check" size="1em" /> How to open BMR Studio

### On the web

1. Open the room at **[app.bettermeetingrooms.com](https://app.bettermeetingrooms.com)**.
2. Go to the **Designs** tab and pick a design — or create one with **New Design**. It opens in BMR Studio, full screen. See [Starting a design](#starting-a-design).

The room's Overview also links to **the active design in the Studio**.

### On iPhone or iPad

1. Open the room in BMR Mobile.
2. Go to **Designs** and open a design.

BMR Mobile opens existing designs. To start a new one in the app, use [AR Design](ar-design-guide).

## <Icon name="star" size="1em" /> Tips

- **Can't select something?** It's in another mode. Tap **Switch**.
- **Review in View**: nothing moves by accident.
- **Start from a scan**: a good scan means less fixing in Structurals.
- **Every Save is a version**: go back from the **Versions** tab.
- **Save your views before a meeting**, then present with **Shift + F**.
