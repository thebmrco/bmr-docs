# <Icon name="marker" size="1em" /> Markers

**Markers** are photos with a short note, placed against the 3D scan of a room. They record the things the mesh alone cannot tell you — where the power is, which display is already mounted, what cabling is in place, where the network points are, and any constraint that will matter later in Design.

A scan captures the *shape* of the room. Markers capture the *context*.

## <Icon name="alert-triangle" size="1em" /> Why markers matter

Markers are one of the most important parts of the Discovery data set. Without them, designers and installers are working from geometry alone and have to guess at everything else.

- **They unblock Design.** A designer looking at the scan in BMR Studio needs to know where the outlet is before they can place a display, and where the existing camera is before they can suggest a replacement. Markers answer those questions without another site visit.
- **They reduce revisions.** Missing markers are the single most common reason a design has to be reworked.
- **They preserve on-site knowledge.** The person doing the scan sees things a designer never will — a loose cable tray, an obstructed wall, a light switch in an awkward place.
- **They speed up installation.** Installers can see, ahead of time, exactly where power, network, and existing equipment are.

## <Icon name="marker" size="1em" /> What to mark

Aim for coverage of the room's *constraints* and *existing equipment*, not decorative detail:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Power | Wall outlets, floor boxes, isolators, spare capacity |
| Network | Data points, patch panels, wireless access points |
| Existing AV | Displays, cameras, microphones, speakers, control panels |
| Cabling & routing | Cable trays, conduits, floor boxes, visible cable runs |
| Structural constraints | Glass walls, mirrors, pillars, low ceilings, HVAC vents |
| Room fixtures | Light switches, blinds/curtain controls, thermostats, door swings |
| Anything unusual | Anything that will surprise a designer who has only seen the scan |

If you find yourself wondering "should I mark this?" — the answer is almost always **yes**. An extra marker costs seconds; a missing marker can cost a site revisit.

:::tip Guided while you scan
While scanning, BMR Mobile shows a **"What to capture" checklist** — power outlets (including floor boxes and behind furniture), network points, existing AV, one overview shot per wall, and the ceiling — counting off each one as you capture it.
:::

## <Icon name="circle-check" size="1em" /> Markers and the room scan

A marker is only anchored in 3D if the app knows where you are standing. That depends on the room having a scan:

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Marking during a scan** | The marker is placed directly in the scan's coordinates. Nothing extra to do. |
| **Adding markers later, room already scanned** | The app **recognises the room** first — point the camera around the space until it matches the saved scan, then capture as usual. |
| **No scan yet** | *Limited mode*: markers are saved as photos without coordinates. The app offers **Scan & Add Markers** so you get both in one pass. |

:::tip Recognising the room on a later visit
When you add markers to an already-scanned room, the app matches the live camera view against the room's saved map — a **"% of room seen"** indicator and directional hints show progress, and a **"Room verified"** confirmation means captured content will sync to this room's scan. If it cannot match the space, you can still **continue without verification**, and the markers are saved without coordinates.
:::

## <Icon name="photo" size="1em" /> Taking a good marker

- **Frame the context, not just the item.** A close-up of a socket without any surrounding wall makes it hard to place later — step back so the wall and nearby features are visible too.
- **Hold steady.** A blurry marker is much less useful than a sharp one. Pause for a moment before capture.
- **Write a short, specific note.** "Floor box — 2× power, 1× data" beats "power".
- **One marker per item.** Don't try to cram several things into a single marker — split them so each can be found and read on its own.
- **Retake if you're not sure.** It is much faster to retake on the spot than to come back later.

## <Icon name="palette" size="1em" /> Where markers show up

Once uploaded, markers become part of the room's **Room Data** and appear:

- In the room's **Markers** list in BMR Mobile and on the web.
- Anchored to the 3D scan in **BMR Studio**, so designers can see each marker in its true position when placing equipment.
- In room-level **Data Completeness** indicators, alongside scan, acoustic measurement, and design status.

Reviewing markers on the web is fast: you can **move straight from one marker to the next**, **view all markers in the room at once**, and reach marker options from the **top menu** rather than hunting per-marker.

Markers also do more than document the room — they help **generate** it. In BMR Studio's Structurals mode, markers are used to determine and apply **surface colour and material** to walls and floors, so the generated design more closely resembles the real space.
