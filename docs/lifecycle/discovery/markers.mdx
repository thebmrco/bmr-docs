# <Icon name="marker" size="1em" /> Markers

**Markers** are photos with a short note, placed against the 3D scan of a room. They record the things the mesh alone cannot tell you — where the power is, which display is already mounted, what cabling is in place, where the network points are, and any constraint that will matter later in Design.

A scan captures the *shape* of the room. Markers capture the *context*.

## <Icon name="alert-triangle" size="1em" /> Why markers matter

Markers are one of the most important parts of the Discovery data set. Without them, designers and installers are working from geometry alone and have to guess at everything else.

- **They unblock Design.** A designer looking at the scan in BMR Studio needs to know where the outlet is before they can place a display, and where the existing camera is before they can suggest a replacement. Markers answer those questions without another site visit.
- **They reduce revisions.** Missing markers are the single most common reason a design has to be reworked.
- **They preserve on-site knowledge.** The person doing the scan sees things a designer never will — a loose cable tray, an obstructed wall, a light switch in an awkward place.
- **They speed up installation.** Installers can see, ahead of time, exactly where power, network, and existing equipment are.

---

## <Icon name="marker" size="1em" /> What to mark

Aim for coverage of the room's *constraints* and *existing equipment*, not decorative detail:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Power | Wall outlets, floor boxes, isolators, spare capacity |
| Network | Data points, patch panels, wireless access points |
| Existing AV | Displays, cameras, microphones, speakers, control panels |
| Cabling & routing | Cable trays, conduits, floor boxes, visible cable runs |
| Structural constraints | Glass walls, mirrors, pillars, low ceilings, HVAC vents |
| Room fixtures | Light switches, blinds/curtain controls, thermostats, door swings |
| Anything unusual | Anything that will surprise a designer who has only seen the scan |

If you find yourself wondering "should I mark this?" — the answer is almost always **yes**. An extra marker costs seconds; a missing marker can cost a site revisit.

:::tip Guided while you scan
While scanning, BMR Mobile shows a **"What to capture" checklist** — power outlets (including floor boxes and behind furniture), network points, existing AV, one overview shot per wall, and the ceiling — counting off each one as you capture it.
:::

---

## <Icon name="palette" size="1em" /> Markers shape the design, not just the record

Markers do more than document the room — they help **generate** it. In BMR Studio's Structurals mode, markers are used to determine and apply **surface colour and material** to walls and floors, so the generated design more closely resembles the real space.
