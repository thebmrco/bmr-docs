# Method

The [Loudspeaker Check](./index.mdx) predicts the signal-to-noise ratio a BMR measurement will reach and compares it
with what the BMR analysis requires. The constants come from the literature, from the BMR analysis, or from BMR's
stored measurements.

## Symbols

| Symbol | Quantity | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| <Tex>{String.raw`L_{1\,\mathrm{m}}`}</Tex> | Datasheet maximum level, referred to 1 m | dB SPL |
| <Tex>{String.raw`L_\mathrm{sw}`}</Tex> | Broadband sweep level at 1 m, full volume | dB SPL |
| <Tex>{String.raw`L_\mathrm{mic}`}</Tex> | Sweep level at the microphone | dB SPL |
| <Tex>{'L_A'}</Tex> | Background noise, A-weighted | dB(A) |
| <Tex>{'L_{N,b}'}</Tex> | Background noise in octave band b | dB SPL |
| <Tex>{String.raw`A,\ V,\ d`}</Tex> | Floor area, volume, source–microphone distance | m², m³, m |
| <Tex>{'T'}</Tex> | Reverberation time | s |
| <Tex>{'Q'}</Tex> | Directivity factor, {DEFAULT_Q} (loudspeaker near a surface) | — |
| <Tex>{String.raw`\mathrm{SNR}_b`}</Tex> | Signal-to-noise ratio in band b | dB |
| <Tex>{String.raw`M,\ \beta,\ \sigma`}</Tex> | Margin to the threshold; borderline zone; uncertainty of the datasheet level | dB |

## What the measurement requires

The BMR analysis needs a minimum signal-to-noise ratio in each octave band. The reverberation time is built from the
500 Hz and 1 kHz bands, so the lower of the two decides.

<TierTable />

{SNR_GATE_DB}, {SNR_CLEAN_DB} and 38 dB are where the BMR analysis acts. {SNR_RELIABLE_DB} dB is this tool's own line:
the minimum plus 3 dB, so that "works" does not mean "only just". Between {SNR_RELIABLE_DB} and {SNR_CLEAN_DB} dB the
analysis may warn that the signal is close to the noise floor. ISO 3382-1 and -2 ask for a decay that starts at least
35 dB above the noise for T20 and 45 dB for T30; that is a level at the start of the decay, which is not the same
quantity as the average signal-to-noise ratio used here. In BMR's stored measurements, T20 between 28 and 35 dB read
about 2 % longer than recordings of the same room at 38 dB or more, within the scatter between those recordings themselves, though that scatter was wider between 31 and 35 dB; two of the three recordings below 28 dB read 18–19 % longer, one about half as long. The {SNR_RELIABLE_DB} dB line is a practical choice, not a
standard.

## Full result: the bands the MOS score needs

The headline room size is the largest room in which the measurement gives the **full result**: 500 Hz and 1 kHz at
{SNR_RELIABLE_DB} dB **and** 125 Hz and 250 Hz at {SNR_GATE_DB} dB. The MOS score's Bass Ratio,
<Tex>{String.raw`(T_{125} + T_{250}) / (T_{500} + T_{1000})`}</Tex>, needs a reverberation time in all four bands, and a
band below {SNR_GATE_DB} dB returns none. Just above that line it can still be missing: in BMR's measurements 125 Hz
returned no reverberation time in about one in four recordings between 25 and 28 dB. The tool keeps its caution in the loudspeaker level and the far-corner microphone instead of adding a third
margin here, so the Bass Ratio is not certain at the headline size: where the tool said a room works, 43 of 54 BMR recordings
got the full result, and the others lost the 125 Hz band.

Loudspeakers arrive weaker in the low bands than a flat model assumes. In BMR's calibrated recordings the median
shortfall against 500 Hz is <LowBandOffsets />. The median is applied to every loudspeaker, and the spread widens the
uncertainty of those two bands.

If 125 Hz or 250 Hz is outside the loudspeaker's range, for example a speakerphone in its voice mode, the MOS score
has no Bass Ratio and the answer says so. The room size stays the one its level allows with that band played:
switching a band off does not make a room bigger.

The range given with the room size shows how far the size moves when every uncertain part (the model's error against
stored measurements, the unknown sweep level, the spread of the low bands) is moved to its cautious or its optimistic
end. It is not a probability. In large rooms a few decibels change the size several-fold, so the range is wide.

The check covers rooms up to <MaxVolume />: DIN 18041 states its A4 target, which lists the video-conference room as an
example, for rooms up to that volume and calls A4 unsuitable above it. As a floor area that is <MaxVolume /> divided by
the ceiling height. The reverberation time is still estimated with the A3 target, as BMR's analysis does.

## From the datasheet to the microphone

<Tex display n="2">{String.raw`L_{1\,\mathrm{m}} = L(d_\mathrm{ref}) + 20\log_{10} d_\mathrm{ref}`}</Tex>
<Tex display n="3">{String.raw`L_\mathrm{sw} = L_{1\,\mathrm{m}} + k, \qquad k = ` + SWEEP_LEVEL_OFFSET_DB + String.raw`\ \mathrm{dB}`}</Tex>

<Tex>{'k'}</Tex> is the level a sine sweep reaches at full volume relative to the maximum-SPL rating, and the least
certain number here. Manufacturer ratings are mostly pink noise at full drive, reported as the highest peak (Weinzierl,
ch. 17); a sweep has a 3 dB crest factor, so crest factor alone would put <Tex>{'k'}</Tex> near −3 dB. In BMR's
recordings of loudspeakers with a published level, played at an unknown volume, the offset per device lay between −15
and 0 dB; a Jabra Speak2 75 about −12 dB. The −12 dB used is a cautious choice from that; a full-volume measurement per
device will replace it.

<Tex display n="4">{'d = \\sqrt{A/2}'}</Tex>

Source centred, microphone in the farthest corner of a square room.

<Tex display n="5">{String.raw`T = a\log_{10} V + b \qquad \text{(DIN 18041, A3: } a = 0.32,\ b = -0.17)`}</Tex>

Used when the reverberation time has not been measured. In BMR rooms the measured value is 0.95 × this target (median over 77 rooms;
half of them lie between 0.78 and 1.12), so real rooms are a little livelier than the target at the low end and a
little drier at the high end.

<Tex display n="6">{String.raw`L_\mathrm{mic} = L_\mathrm{sw} - 10\log_{10}\frac{Q}{4\pi} + 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{Q}{4\pi d^2} + \frac{25\,T}{V}\right)`}</Tex>

Direct sound plus the reverberant field. Both reach the microphone, so both count. Where only the microphone moved,
BMR's measured level fell far less with distance than the direct sound alone predicts (slope near 0 against −1).
Equation (6) predicts −0.3 to −0.5 there, so its distance dependence is not confirmed either.

## The SNR the measurement will reach

<Tex display n="7">{'L_{N,b} = L_A + \\Delta N_b'}</Tex>

<NoiseOffsetTable />

Median over 157 calibrated BMR measurements; room noise is highest in the low bands.

<Tex display n="8">{String.raw`\mathrm{SNR}_b = L_\mathrm{mic} - L_{N,b} + G, \qquad G = ` + SWEEP_ANALYSIS_GAIN_DB.toFixed(1) + String.raw`\ \mathrm{dB}`}</Tex>

<Tex>{'G'}</Tex> is a fixed property of the BMR measurement signal and its analysis, checked on simulated rooms to within
±1 dB from 500 Hz to 8 kHz.

<Tex display n="9">{String.raw`M = \min(\mathrm{SNR}_{500}, \mathrm{SNR}_{1000}) - \mathrm{SNR}_\mathrm{req}`}</Tex>
<Tex display n="10">{String.raw`\text{works if } M \ge \beta, \quad \text{borderline if } |M| < \beta, \quad \text{does not work if } M \le -\beta, \qquad \beta = ` + PREDICTION_UNCERTAINTY_DB.toFixed(1) + String.raw`\ \mathrm{dB}`}</Tex>

<Tex>{'\\beta'}</Tex> is how far the prediction can be trusted: {PREDICTION_UNCERTAINTY_DB.toFixed(1)} dB. It combines
the model's error against stored measurements (4.5 dB) with the unknown sweep offset <Tex>{'k'}</Tex>, which in BMR's
recordings lay between −15 and 0 dB per device (4.5 dB if every value in that range is equally likely). It shrinks once
<Tex>{'k'}</Tex> is measured per device. <Tex>{'\\sigma'}</Tex> is subtracted from the loudspeaker's level when the datasheet leaves something open: 3 dB when it omits the measuring distance, 2–4 dB when the level is derived from a wattage. Nothing is subtracted from the prediction.

For the Jabra Speak2 75 in the 28 m² Aquarium (37–39 dB(A), T = 0.45 s) the tool predicts about 33 dB at 500 Hz; the
two recordings measured 30.7 and 31.5 dB, both below the 35 dB warning line. That agrees, but it is not
an independent check: <Tex>{'k'}</Tex> was chosen with the same recordings in view. Of the 77 rooms with a stored SNR, 7 are
larger than {FEW_MEASUREMENTS_ABOVE_M2} m².

"No noise warning" means none in the 500 Hz and 1 kHz bands. Other bands can still warn, most often 125 Hz, and the
analysis is stricter when the microphone is very close to the loudspeaker.

The largest floor area for a tier is the <Tex>{'A'}</Tex> at which <Tex>{'M = 0'}</Tex>. Beyond the reverberation
radius the level falls only with <Tex>{String.raw`10\log_{10}(T/V)`}</Tex>, so a few decibels change that area
several-fold: treat it as a guide and the verdict for the actual room as the answer.

## Frequency coverage

A datasheet's frequency limit is where the output is down by a few dB (usually 3, sometimes 10), not where it stops.
A band therefore counts when its centre <Tex>{'f_c'}</Tex> lies inside the stated range, and is marked partial when the
limit falls between <Tex>{'f_c/\\sqrt{2}'}</Tex> and <Tex>{'f_c\\sqrt{2}'}</Tex>: measured, but weaker. In BMR's
measurements, room systems rated from 100 Hz reached 24–34 dB in the 125 Hz band, and a speakerphone rated from 250 Hz
3–23 dB. A partial band on a driver smaller than 60 mm is flagged, because that is where the band really does run out.
The BMR app reports 125 Hz – 8 kHz ({REQUIRED_SPAN_HZ[0].toFixed(1)} – {REQUIRED_SPAN_HZ[1].toFixed(0)} Hz); its bands
are {CENTER_FREQUENCIES.join(', ')} Hz, and 63 Hz is not measured because the sweep starts at 80 Hz.

## What it does not include

- The loudspeaker's own frequency response and directivity, beyond the typical low-band shortfall above.
- Rooms that are not diffuse, where the model behind equation (6) does not hold. EN 12354-6 §5 notes that for its own
  absorption model the real reverberation time can then be up to twice the prediction.
- Rooms that are long and narrow. The tool assumes a square room.
- The volume actually set, limiters, and battery state. This is what <Tex>{'k'}</Tex> stands in for.
