# <Icon name="audio-lines" size="1em" /> Understanding the MOS Score

<PageIntro illustration="acoustics" meta={['BMR Mobile', 'Web platform']}>

One number from **1 to 5**: how easy it is to talk and listen in the room. **Good from 3.6.**

</PageIntro>

<Notice kind="acoustics" />

## <Icon name="help-circle" size="1em" /> The scale {#-what-is-a-mos-score}

<MosScaleVisual />

| Score | Label | In a meeting |
|---|---|---|
| 4.6 – 5.0 | **Excellent** | Effortless |
| 3.6 – 4.5 | **Good** | Easy. Fine for hybrid meetings |
| 3.0 – 3.5 | **Fair** | Takes effort. In-person only |
| 1.0 – 2.9 | **Poor** | Tiring. Treat the room |

**MOS** means **Mean Opinion Score**. It measures **listening effort**, not just whether you can hear the words.

---

## <Icon name="sliders-horizontal" size="1em" /> How the score is made {#how-the-score-is-made}

Three parts, added up:

| | Part | Range | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| | **Reverberation** | 1 to 5 | How long sound lingers. The base of the score |
| **+** | **Bass balance** | a little up or down | Boomy bass lowers it, tight bass helps a little |
| **−** | **Speech-to-noise** | 0 or less | Background noise (fans, HVAC, hallway) takes points off |
| **=** | **MOS** | 1 to 5 | |

You see all three as bars next to the score on the web. The two rooms below show how they add up.

### What made the difference {#what-made-the-difference}

| | Room A | Room B |
|---|---|---|
| **Average RT60** | 1.16 s, far above the band | 0.25 s, in the band |
| **Reverberation** | 1.1 | 5.0 |
| **Bass balance** | +0.22 | 0.00 |
| **Ambient noise** | 28 dB(A) | 23 dB(A) |
| **Speech-to-noise** | 0.00 | −0.13 |
| **MOS** | **1.3 Poor** | **4.9 Excellent** |

**Reverberation decides most of the score.** Noise matters most once a room is already good: quieter Room B lost points to noise, Room A had nothing left to lose.

## <Icon name="target" size="1em" /> The Green Zone {#-the-green-zone}

The green band on the RT60 chart is the **DIN 18041** target for a room of this size and use. On the web: **DIN 18041 tolerance bands**, with the target as a dashed line.

| The curve is | Means |
|---|---|
| **Inside** the band | On target |
| **Above** | Too reverberant. Speech blurs, effort rises (Room A) |
| **Below** | Overdamped. Can feel dry or muffled |

In BMR Mobile, a band left out of the result is shaded and labelled **Invalid measurement**.

## <Icon name="circle-check" size="1em" /> What to aim for {#-what-a-good-score-looks-like}

| Use | Aim for |
|---|---|
| **Hybrid meetings** | **Good**, 3.6 or more. Remote people notice the room most |
| **In-person only** | **Fair**, 3.0 or more, is workable |
| **Any room scoring Poor** | Treat it: absorption, furniture, or less noise |

Measure **three spots**, far apart and at least 1 m from walls and the speaker. The score is their average.

---

## <Icon name="star" size="1em" /> The MOS variants {#the-mos-variants}

The label under the score says which parts are in it.

| Label | Includes | Since |
|---|---|---|
| **RT** | Reverberation | Base |
| **RT+BR** | + Bass balance | Release 1.2 |
| **RT+BR+N** | + Speech-to-noise | Release 2.0 |

New measurements with the current BMR Mobile are **RT+BR+N**. Older ones keep their label. If the noise reading can't be trusted, a score stays **RT+BR**.

## <Icon name="scan" size="1em" /> The parts in more detail {#-what-the-score-is-based-on}

The score comes from research with the **Institute of Electronic Music and Acoustics** at the University of Music and Performing Arts Graz.

### Reverberation time {#reverberation-time-t60}

How long sound lingers after it stops. Short is clear; long blurs words together. It sets the base of the score (Room A: 1.16 s → 1.1; Room B: 0.25 s → 5.0).

### Bass balance {#bass-ratio}

Reverberation in the bass compared with the voice range: `(T125 + T250) / (T500 + T1000)`.

| In the app | Bass Ratio | Effect on the score |
|---|---|---|
| **Tight** | Below about 1.15 | Up a little. The only part that can raise the score |
| **Balanced** | About 1.15 | None |
| **Boomy** | Above about 1.15 | Down. The boomier, the more |

### Speech-to-noise {#speech-to-noise-ratio}

Normal speech compared with the steady background noise.

| Gap between speech and noise | Feels like | Penalty in an excellent room |
|---|---|---|
| 40 dB or more | Very quiet | None |
| About 30 dB | Soft HVAC | Noticeable |
| About 20 dB | Audible fan | Large |
| 10–15 dB | Noisy HVAC, hallway | Very large |
| A few dB | Noise as loud as speech | Down to the bottom |

#### Why noise hurts good rooms more {#why-noise-hurts-good-rooms-more}

A good room has more to lose. Room B lost 0.13 at 23 dB(A); Room A, at 28 dB(A), lost nothing. Treating the echo without the noise can leave points on the table.

#### How BMR measures it {#how-bmr-measures-it}

From the short quiet moment **just before the sweep**, after the **3-2-1** countdown.

:::caution Stay quiet after the 3-2-1
Talking, moving chairs or an open door then lowers the score. A single short noise is filtered out; a steady one is not.
:::

### Spatial concentration {#direct-to-reverberant-ratio-drr}

Direct sound compared with reflected sound. **Not yet in the score**, and not shown.

### Extra metrics {#additional-metrics-xi20-and-xi30}

| Where | Shown |
|---|---|
| Web | RT60 (T20, or T30), Clarity (C50), Definition (D50), SNR, Ambient Noise, Centre Time |
| BMR Mobile, **Expert Mode** | T20, ambient noise, C50, D50, centre time, SNR, Xi20 (how well T20 fits; lower is better) |

### Intelligibility vs. listening effort {#-intelligibility-vs-listening-effort}

In most meeting rooms the words are audible. The problem is the **effort** to follow them. An echoey room can be perfectly audible and still leave people drained after an hour, remote participants most of all. Classic metrics were made for halls; the MOS score is made for meetings.

## <Icon name="alert-triangle" size="1em" /> What the score does not cover {#-what-the-score-does-not-cover}

- Microphone and speaker quality.
- Network and video-call compression.

Spatial concentration and flutter echo are planned for a later version.
