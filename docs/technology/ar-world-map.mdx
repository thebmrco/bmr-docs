# <Icon name="target" size="1em" /> AR World Map

A single room collects many kinds of data over time — a 3D scan, markers, acoustic measurements, and AR designs — often captured on different visits. For all of it to line up in 3D, every capture has to share **one coordinate system**. This page explains how BMR keeps a room's data aligned, why the app **detects the room** before you place anything new, and what to do when detection fails.

:::info AR World Map — "AR Map" for short
The **AR World Map** is the set of visual feature points that lets a device recognise a room it has seen before. It is created by the **room scan** — no scan, no AR Map. BMR Mobile shows it as **AR Map** on the room page.
:::

---

## <Icon name="room" size="1em" /> One coordinate space per room

**Everything starts with the [room scan](../lifecycle/discovery/room-scan).** The first LiDAR scan of a room does two things at once:

1. It builds the **3D model** of the room.
2. It establishes the room's **coordinate space** — an origin and axes — and saves the **AR World Map**: the visual feature points a device uses to recognise the room on a later visit.

Both come from that one scan. Until a room has been scanned it has no coordinate space and no AR Map, so nothing captured in it can be positioned in 3D.

Everything captured afterwards is **anchored to that same space**. A marker's `x, y, z`, an acoustic measurement's position, and an AR-placed display all refer to the same origin — which is exactly why they can be shown together, compared, and combined in BMR Studio.

## <Icon name="scan" size="1em" /> Why the app detects the room first

When you start a new **AR Design** — or add a **marker**, or run an **acoustic measurement** — the device has no idea where it is relative to the room's saved AR Map. It first has to **relocalize**: match what the camera sees now to the feature points stored during the original scan, and from that recover the room's coordinate space.

Only once that match succeeds can new data be placed in the **same** coordinate space as everything else. Skip it, and a new item would sit in an unrelated coordinate system — misaligned with the scan and every other capture.

## <Icon name="alert-triangle" size="1em" /> Why detection sometimes fails

Detection works by matching **visual features**, so anything that makes the room look different from the original scan makes a match harder to find:

## <Icon name="circle-check" size="1em" /> Getting a reliable match

- **Start where the scan started** — stand roughly where the original scan began and face a similar direction.
- **Aim at feature-rich areas** — corners, furniture, fixtures and posters match well; blank walls, glass and mirrors don't.
- **Move slowly and steadily** — give the device a few seconds of gentle movement rather than a quick sweep.
- **Match the lighting** — recreate the original conditions where you can (same blinds, same lights on), and add light to a dark room.
- **Hold steady** — avoid fast motion so the image stays sharp.

---

## <Icon name="info" size="1em" /> If it still can't find the room

:::tip You have three options
- **Retry** — move to a more recognisable part of the room (with more visual detail) and try again.
- **Continue without verification** — you can still place items, but they may not align precisely with the existing scan and data. Best kept for quick visualisation only.
- **Re-scan the room** — if the space has changed substantially (renovation, new furniture, a repaint), the stored AR Map no longer matches. A fresh scan re-establishes the coordinate space for everything that follows.
:::

## <Icon name="puzzle" size="1em" /> Where this applies

Room detection runs whenever new spatial data is added to a scanned room, so it stays aligned with everything already captured:

- Starting an **[AR Design](../lifecycle/design/ar-design-guide)**
- Adding a **marker**
- Running an **acoustic measurement**
